DRISHTI — Dead-Reckoning with Intelligent Sensor-fusion for High-integrity Terrestrial Inertial Navigation

When GNSS disappears, navigation shouldn't.

DRISHTI is our AI-ML based intelligent dead-reckoning system for seamless navigation during GNSS-denied conditions, developed for Smart India Hackathon 2026, Problem Statement SIH26168 by ISRO.

In this 3-minute prototype demonstration, we show how DRISHTI is designed to allow a smartphone to continue estimating vehicle position when GNSS becomes unavailable in environments such as tunnels, underpasses, basements and dense urban areas.

HOW DRISHTI WORKS

• Smartphone IMU sensors — accelerometer, gyroscope and magnetometer
• AI-based forward-speed estimation
• Error-State Kalman Filter
• Non-Holonomic Constraints
• Zero-Velocity Updates
• Offline OpenStreetMap-based map matching
• GNSS integrity monitoring
• Seamless GNSS recovery
• Uncertainty and navigation integrity estimation

Our core philosophy:

"AI learns the motion. Physics owns the position. The road keeps it honest."

PROTOTYPE DEMONSTRATION

The demo shows a vehicle transitioning from normal GNSS navigation into a GNSS-denied environment.

During the outage:

🔴 Conventional inertial dead reckoning accumulates error and drifts away from the road.

🔵 DRISHTI continues estimating vehicle motion using learned speed, physical constraints, filtering and map information.

When GNSS becomes available again, DRISHTI performs a soft handover instead of abruptly restarting or snapping the position.

EVALUATION

Our target is less than 10 percent positioning drift during GNSS-denied operation.

We plan to evaluate DRISHTI using:

• Position and trajectory error
• Drift percentage
• Heading drift
• GNSS outage duration
• Cross-device performance
• Different phone mounting positions
• Tunnels and underpasses
• Parking structures
• Potholes and vibration
• GNSS failure scenarios
• Unseen routes and devices

The system is designed for on-device operation without requiring cloud connectivity during a GNSS outage, with a future path toward deployment using higher-grade IMU hardware.
